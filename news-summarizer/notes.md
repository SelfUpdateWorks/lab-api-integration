- Unfaithful summary: the BBC summary stated "97.4% of participants administered shocks" – this number was NOT in the article text. The model added it from its own training knowledge (Google's AI overview links a similar figure to other modern studies). Even if true elsewhere, attributing it to this article is a faithfulness error – the most dangerous kind, because exact numbers sound credible.
- Derived number: the first summary said "97.4% complied", but the article only said "2.6% of 800 refused". The model computed 100 − 2.6 and presented the result as a stated fact. After adding "Use ONLY facts stated in the text", the summary used the article's own numbers (2.6%, 800) and the number check found nothing to flag.
- Lesson: a number can be logically correct and still be unfaithful to the source. My code check would flag a derived number like 97.4 because it is not in the text.
